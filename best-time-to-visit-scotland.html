Best Time to Visit Scotland for a Road Trip

Weather, crowds, midges, and daylight - broken down by season.

The Sweet Spot: Late May-June and September

These windows give you the best combination of long daylight hours, decent odds of good weather, and noticeably lighter crowds than peak July-August. May-June also sits just before Highland midges become a serious nuisance, and September catches the tail end of good weather after the worst of the midge season has passed, with the added bonus of early autumn colour starting to show in the glens.

Season by Season

Spring (March-May)

Quieter roads and lower prices, but weather is the most unpredictable of the year - snow is still possible on higher passes into April. Late May starts to feel like the real sweet spot as daylight stretches out.

Summer (June-August)

The longest daylight hours of the year (up to 18 hours in the far north in June) and the warmest weather, but also peak crowds, peak accommodation prices, and peak midge season - especially July and August near lochs and woodland at dawn/dusk.

Autumn (September-November)

September is excellent - still-decent weather, thinning crowds, and midges largely gone. October brings genuinely spectacular autumn colour in the glens, though days shorten quickly and weather turns more changeable. November tips into off-season conditions.

Winter (December-February)

Dramatic, often snow-dusted scenery and a real chance of seeing the Northern Lights from the far north on a clear night - but very short daylight hours (as little as 6-7 hours in December), some Highland roads and passes affected by snow, and many smaller attractions and B&Bs closed for the season.

Two Dates Worth Planning Around

  • Edinburgh Festival Fringe (August): incredible if that's the trip you want, but Edinburgh accommodation gets both scarce and expensive - book months ahead if your route starts or ends there in August.
  • School summer holidays (roughly July-August): the single biggest driver of domestic UK crowding on popular routes like the NC500 and Skye - a factor even beyond the general summer tourist season.
