NC500 Clockwise or Anticlockwise: Which Direction Is Better?

The North Coast 500 can be driven in either direction, but clockwise and anticlockwise routes create different driving flows and experiences. Here's how to choose between them.

The Short Answer

Both directions see similar overall traffic, and neither is objectively "correct." The real difference is pacing and emotional arc: anticlockwise (starting west, through Applecross) front-loads the most dramatic single-track driving and scenery early in the trip, while clockwise (starting north up the east coast) eases you in gently before the wilder western stretches arrive later. Which one suits you depends on how you like a trip to build.

The Case for Each Direction

Anticlockwise (West First)

You hit Applecross and the most dramatic single-track driving of the whole route in the first day or two, while energy and excitement are highest. Some drivers prefer getting the most technically demanding driving out of the way early, rather than facing it while already tired later in the trip.

Clockwise (East First)

The east coast is gentler and faster driving, letting you build confidence before the tighter, more demanding roads of the west and Applecross arrive later. If it's your first time on single-track roads, easing in this way can feel less daunting.

Practical Factors Worth Considering

  • Sun position for photos: the west coast's best light is generally in the afternoon and evening, so driving it anticlockwise (arriving west coast spots earlier in the day) versus clockwise (arriving in late afternoon/evening) can meaningfully change your photos.
  • Prevailing wind: Scotland's weather tends to move west to east. Driving anticlockwise can mean facing incoming weather on the west coast before it clears, while clockwise sometimes means chasing clearer skies as they move east - though this varies day to day and isn't a reliable rule.
  • Traffic patterns: since most visitors don't strongly favour one direction over the other, you won't reliably avoid crowds either way - but starting early in the day at popular stops like Applecross helps regardless of direction.
